Getting ones onto the diagonal

network flow matching

Let M be an n × n matrix whose entries are all 0 or 1, and write mij for the entry in row i, column j. A diagonal entry is one of the form mii.

Swapping rows i and j means exchanging mik and mjk for every k; swapping two columns is the same idea. We call M rearrangeable if some sequence of row swaps and column swaps leaves every diagonal entry equal to 1.

(a) Give a matrix that is not rearrangeable even though every row and every column contains at least one 1. (b) Give a polynomial-time algorithm that decides whether a 0-1 matrix is rearrangeable.

Claim one 1 in each row, never two in the same column. Fill every row and the matrix can be rearranged to put your claims on the diagonal. The model checks your claims and nothing else; it will not pick any for you.

Questions to sit with

  1. Rearrange Example 1, then clear it and rearrange it a different way. Ignore the swapping entirely for a moment: in plain terms, what is the thing you are choosing when you rearrange a matrix?
  2. Argue that you never have to reason about the order of the swaps at all. Why does any amount of row and column swapping come down to picking one 1 in each row with no two in the same column?
  3. Example 2 has a 1 in every row and in every column, and still cannot be rearranged, which answers part (a). Find the rows that spoil it. How many rows are in that set, and how many columns do all of their 1s fit into?
  4. State the general condition. For which sets of rows does the presence of such a set make a matrix impossible to rearrange, and why is that obviously fatal?
  5. Example 3 can be rearranged in exactly one way, even though it has fifteen 1s spread about. How many ways are there of picking one 1 per row before you worry about collisions? Why is trying them all a bad plan for a matrix of size n?
  6. Draw the bipartite graph with a node per row, a node per column, and an edge for every 1. What are you hunting for in that graph? Now turn it into a flow network: where do the source and sink go, what are the capacities, and what value of the maximum flow means yes?
  7. When the answer is no, the blocking set of rows is a certificate that somebody else can check quickly. Say what that set corresponds to in your flow network, and which theorem guarantees one always exists when the answer is no.
  8. Someone hands you a finished rearrangement and claims it works. How long does checking take? Compare that with deciding the question from scratch.
