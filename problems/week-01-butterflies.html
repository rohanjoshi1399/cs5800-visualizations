Butterfly species judgments

graphs labeling

Your friends have taken up amateur lepidoptery. They return from a trip with n butterflies, each belonging to one of two species, A or B. They cannot label any single specimen directly.

Instead, for each pair of specimens they study the two side by side. If they are confident, they record the pair as "same" or "different." If not, they record nothing and the pair is ambiguous. This leaves n specimens and m judgments.

The judgments are consistent if it is possible to label each specimen A or B such that every pair marked "same" gets two matching labels and every pair marked "different" gets two differing labels. Determine whether the m judgments are consistent, in O(m + n) time.

Use the model below to get a feel for the problem. Assign a species to each butterfly and watch which judgments hold. The model does not run any algorithm; it only reports what your own assignment does.

Questions to sit with

  1. Load Example 1 and find an assignment where every judgment holds. Now find a second one. How are the two related, and does that relationship always happen?
  2. Once you have fixed the species of one butterfly, how much of the rest is still up to you?
  3. Try Example 2. When you cannot satisfy everything, what exactly is stopping you? Point to the specific set of judgments responsible, not just the butterfly that lights up.
  4. Example 3 has a group of butterflies with no judgments connecting it to the others. Does that change whether the whole set is consistent?
  5. In build mode, construct the smallest set of judgments you can that is impossible to satisfy. Then make another that is impossible for a different reason, if you can.
  6. Suppose someone hands you a complete assignment and claims it works. How much work does it take you to check their claim? Is checking cheaper than finding one?
